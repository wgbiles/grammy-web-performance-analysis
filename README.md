# 📊 Case Study: Web Performance Analysis & UX Experimentation

## 🎯 Executive Summary
A comprehensive web analytics evaluation was conducted to determine if separating our digital ecosystem into two distinct platforms—the consumer-focused Grammys site (Grammy.com) and the B2B Recording Academy (TRA) site—improved user engagement. The analysis confirms that the split successfully eliminated audience dilution by aligning user traffic with targeted environments. This case study details the platform engagement profiles, seasonal traffic surges, and an executive recommendation for mobile optimization.

---

## 📈 Key Findings & Performance Metrics

### 1. Platform-Specific Engagement Divergence
* **TRA Standalone Site:** User engagement metrics improved significantly. The bounce rate dropped by **~8% (down to 33.7%)**, and the average time on site increased by **26 seconds**, reaching an average of **128.5 seconds** per session.
* **Grammys Standalone Site:** The bounce rate experienced a negligible drop of less than **1% (remaining at 40.2%)**, while the average session duration compressed by approximately **20 seconds** (dropping to **83.0 seconds**).

### 2. Universal Depth Improvements
* The average pages per session (PPS) increased across both independent sites compared to the original combined platform, climbing from **1.86 PPS (combined)** to **2.25 PPS (Grammys)** and **2.78 PPS (TRA)**.

### 3. Traffic Profiles & Device Distribution
* **Event-Driven Surges:** Highly concentrated traffic spikes continue to occur across both platforms during core lifecycle milestones, specifically around annual nominee announcements and Grammy Show nights.
* **Mobile Dominance:** Post-split audience demographics show that **73.7% of all visitors** to the standalone Grammys site utilize a mobile device.

---

## 🧠 Strategic Data Interpretation
The variance in engagement metrics between the two standalone platforms is directly linked to the underlying device profiles. The lower session duration and flat bounce rate on Grammy.com correspond with a platform where nearly three-quarters of the audience engages via mobile devices. Mobile browsing is structurally characterized by shorter session lengths and higher bounce rates due to cellular network performance and on-the-go usage. 

Conversely, the distinct metric improvements on the TRA site reflect a cleaner, friction-free ecosystem once consumer traffic was removed from the environment.

---

## 🔬 UX Experimentation (A/B Test Results)
A controlled UX experiment was evaluated to track homepage Call-to-Action (CTA) interactions (*"Click here to watch highlights from the 64th GRAMMY Awards!"*) across a sample size of **7,896 users**. 
* **Performance:** The split website structure (Test Group) out-performed the combined legacy site (Control Group) by a **1% conversion lift**.
* **Statistical Significance:** The testing calculator demonstrated a **96% confidence level**, surpassing the standard **95% statistical significance threshold**. This proves the new split architecture natively drives higher user interaction.

---

## 🚀 Strategic Recommendations
1. **Maintain Site Separation:** Do not merge the platforms back together. Reunifying the sites would reintroduce audience friction and directly reverse the documented metric optimization achieved on the standalone TRA property.
2. **Implement Mobile Performance Optimization:** Because 73.7% of consumer traffic is mobile, developmental resources must shift to maximize cellular performance on Grammy.com. Priorities must focus on minimizing page load times over cellular networks, streamlining mobile navigation layouts, and reducing technical barriers to encourage deeper interaction from mobile visitors.
